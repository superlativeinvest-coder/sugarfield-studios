# Sugarfield Studio — Funnel Psychology Research
*Why funnels work, why premium ladders require different psychology, and how the evidence applies to the upgraded Pokopia funnel*

# 1. Canonical Funnel Used by This Research

```text
FREE STARTER
    ↓
DAILY RESET — $4.99
    ↓
ADVENTURE COMPANION — $19.99
    ↓
COMPLETE PLAYER SYSTEM — $49
    ↓
MASTER COLLECTION — $79–$99
```

The older commercial ladder using `$3.99`, `$7.99`, and `$14.99` is deprecated.

Do not use the old funnel as current offer truth.

This research file does not invent technical route names, SKUs, Stripe Price
IDs, bundle contents, or entitlement mappings. Codex must read the current live
implementation and product truth for those details.

---

# 2. Executive Conclusion

Funnels work best when they make a sequence of decisions easier than one giant
decision.

The strongest research-backed mechanisms relevant to Sugarfield are:

1. friction reduction
2. uncertainty reduction
3. small voluntary commitment
4. goal progress
5. guided choice
6. trust
7. reference points
8. psychological ownership
9. relevant social proof
10. problem sequencing
11. behavior-based segmentation
12. measurement and iteration

The upgraded funnel adds a crucial requirement:

> The psychology that sells a `$4.99` tool is not the same psychology that sells
> a `$49` system or a `$79–$99` premium collection.

As price rises, the buyer needs stronger evidence of scope, relevance,
differentiation, trust, completeness, and value relative to the prior tier.

# 3. Funnel Psychology by Price Stage

## Free → $4.99
Decision type: **trust conversion**

Buyer question:
```text
"Was the free value useful enough that I trust the paid tool?"
```

Main mechanisms:
- uncertainty reduction
- behavioral momentum
- familiarity
- low friction

---

## $4.99 → $19.99
Decision type: **value expansion**

Buyer question:
```text
"Is this enough more useful to justify the higher spend?"
```

Main mechanisms:
- demonstrated value
- trust transfer
- broader outcome
- contextual comparison
- reduced fragmentation

---

## $19.99 → $49
Decision type: **system purchase**

Buyer question:
```text
"Do I want individual tools, or the complete core system?"
```

Main mechanisms:
- integration
- scope
- reference value
- identity
- perceived completeness
- reduced effort

---

## $49 → $79–$99
Decision type: **premium collection decision**

Buyer question:
```text
"Why should I own the broadest collection instead of stopping at the core system?"
```

Main mechanisms:
- collection identity
- psychological ownership
- premium differentiation
- completeness
- reference points
- trust
- real proof

# 4. Friction Kills Conversion

Baymard's checkout research documents abandonment reasons including complicated
checkout, forced account creation, trust concerns, errors, surprise costs, and
lack of preferred payment methods.

Stripe has also published experimental evidence that relevant payment-method
presentation can improve conversion.

Sugarfield rule:

**Every funnel step must earn its existence.**

A page should:
- reduce uncertainty
- explain a next problem
- qualify the buyer
- deliver value
- complete payment
- fulfill purchase
- present a relevant next offer

Do not make the funnel longer simply because the prices are higher.

---

# 5. Free Value Reduces Uncertainty

Research on free sampling supports uncertainty reduction as an important
mechanism.

The Free Starter should prove:
- organization quality
- visual quality
- ease of use
- usefulness
- trustworthiness

Desired transition:

```text
"This is useful."
        ↓
"Would I pay $4.99 for the complete daily tool?"
```

---

# 6. Small Commitments Can Build Momentum

Foot-in-the-door research suggests small voluntary actions can sometimes make
related later actions easier.

Do not treat this as an automatic sales law.

Use it as:

```text
Useful content
↓
Free Starter
↓
Use it
↓
Daily Reset purchase
↓
Experience paid value
↓
Consider Adventure Companion
```

---

# 7. Progress Can Increase Persistence

Goal-gradient and endowed-progress research show that perceived progress can
affect persistence.

Apply transparently:

```text
START · 1 — 2 — 3 — 4 · COMPLETE
```

Use real state only.

Progress should answer:
- Where am I?
- How much is left?
- What happens next?

---

# 8. Choice Overload Is Contextual

A major meta-analysis found the average choice-overload effect was near zero
across studied conditions, with substantial variability.

Therefore:

```text
"Fewer choices always convert better"
```

is not a scientific law.

Use **guided choice**:
- one recommended action
- one easy decline
- little unrelated clutter

---

# 9. Defaults Influence Decisions

Defaults can influence behavior through effort, implied endorsement, and
reference dependence.

Use recommendation framing, not hidden paid defaults.

Good:
```text
RECOMMENDED NEXT
Adventure Companion — $19.99
Best for players who want broader organization after Daily Reset.
```

Bad:
- prechecked upsells
- auto-added products
- hidden opt-out

---

# 10. Psychological Ownership

Meta-analytic evidence supports a mere-ownership effect.

Ethical Sugarfield application:

```text
Download your Quick Start.
Print your Daily Reset.
Use your Adventure Companion.
Build your Sugarfield system.
```

At premium level, collection ownership can become meaningful.

Do not imply ownership before purchase.

---

# 11. Reference Points Shape Value

The upgraded ladder naturally creates references:

```text
$4.99
$19.99
$49
$79–$99
```

Use honest tier distinctions.

Do not create fake crossed-out anchors or fictional retail prices.

---

# 12. Social Proof Reduces Uncertainty

Real reviews matter more as price rises.

At `$4.99`, strong product previews may be enough.

At `$49` or `$79–$99`, real proof becomes more valuable.

Never create:
- fake reviews
- fake stars
- fake sales counts
- fake testimonials
- fake bestseller labels

Before real proof exists, use:
- product truth
- exact previews
- strong demonstrations
- transparent scope
- FAQ

---

# 13. Trust Is a Conversion Variable

At purchase the customer asks:

```text
Do I want this?
AND
Do I trust this transaction?
```

Trust comes from:
- Stripe
- consistent branding
- exact pricing
- real previews
- exact deliverables
- secure access
- no surprise charges
- clear fan-made positioning
- working fulfillment/support

Higher prices require stronger trust.

---

# 14. Sequential Upsells Work Through Problem Sequencing

Best logic:

```text
"You solved X.
The next problem is Y.
This solves Y."
```

Applied:

## Free Starter → Daily Reset
Solved: getting started  
Next: maintaining a repeatable daily routine

## Daily Reset → Adventure Companion
Solved: daily routine  
Next: broader gameplay organization

## Adventure Companion → Complete Player System
Solved: broader companion need  
Next: fragmentation across player needs

## Complete Player System → Master Collection
Solved: core system  
Next desire: broadest approved collection

The final transition includes both problem solving and collection identity.

# 16. Why Premium Pricing Requires Stronger Differentiation

The jump from `$19.99` to `$49` changes the decision from:

```text
"Should I add this?"
```

to:

```text
"Should I buy the system?"
```

The jump from `$49` to `$79–$99` asks:

```text
"Do I want the premium collection?"
```

Premium pages need to answer:
- Why is this not redundant?
- What does it replace?
- What becomes easier?
- What is included?
- What is exclusive to this tier?
- Who is this for?
- Who should stop at the lower tier?

---

# 17. Premium Offer Clarity Rule

Do not differentiate higher offers only by page count.

Weak:
```text
19 pages vs 37 pages
```

Stronger:
```text
Adventure Companion = broader companion
Complete Player System = coordinated core system
Master Collection = broadest approved collection
```

Only claim exact modules, bonuses, products, or page counts after verifying
current product truth.

# 18. Page-by-Page Psychology

## Page 1 — Free Starter
Job:
- reduce uncertainty
- earn first commitment
- demonstrate quality
- orient the user

Desired thought:
```text
"This looks useful and safe to try."
```

## Page 2 — Daily Reset $4.99
Job:
Create the first paid transaction.

Desired thought:
```text
"For $4.99, this solves a recurring problem."
```

## Page 3 — Adventure Companion $19.99
Job:
Prove a meaningful jump in scope.

Desired thought:
```text
"This is not just another checklist. It's a broader companion."
```

## Page 4 — Complete Player System $49
Job:
Sell integration and systemization.

Desired thought:
```text
"I want the full core system instead of piecing things together."
```

## Page 5 — Master Collection $79–$99
Job:
Sell premium completeness.

Desired thought:
```text
"I want the broadest approved collection."
```

# 19. Price-Ladder Psychology

| Transition | Psychological Job |
|---|---|
| Free → $4.99 | Trust |
| $4.99 → $19.99 | Expansion |
| $19.99 → $49 | System |
| $49 → $79–$99 | Premium completeness |

The ladder creates structure only if value expands with price.

# 20. Clear Decline Paths Support Trust

Optional upsells need a visible decline.

Never:
- hide "No thanks"
- make it tiny
- guilt the user
- block earned access
- repeat the same declined offer repeatedly

Premium selling should feel confident, not coercive.

---

# 21. Fulfillment Before Reselling

Immediately after purchase, show:
- payment success
- exact purchase
- download/access
- what to do next
- support

Then present the next optional offer.

This becomes more important at `$49` and `$79–$99`.

---

# 22. The Free Starter Should Give a Complete Small Win

Bad:
```text
"Here is half the answer. Pay for the rest."
```

Better:
```text
"Here is a complete Quick Start.
If you want the recurring daily system, Daily Reset is next."
```

---

# 23. Scarcity and Urgency

Most Sugarfield products are digital.

Avoid:
```text
ONLY 3 LEFT
SELLING OUT
LAST COPY
```

Use urgency only when real:
- actual promotion end
- verified event deadline
- real launch window
- real update date

Never invent scarcity to justify premium pricing.

---

# 24. Product Preview Psychology

Digital buyers cannot physically inspect the product.

Therefore accurate previews carry a large trust burden.

Canonical rule:

```text
VISIBLE PRODUCT PREVIEW = APPROVED CUSTOMER ART
```

This is especially important for `$49` and `$79–$99`.

# 28. Evidence Strength

## Strong / High Utility
- checkout friction
- progress / goal gradient
- defaults / choice architecture
- framing / reference dependence
- free sampling as uncertainty reduction
- psychological ownership

## Useful but Context Dependent
- choice overload
- reciprocity
- social proof
- decoy effects
- scarcity effects

## Do Not Treat as Scientific Law
- "7 touchpoints"
- "red buttons convert best"
- "three pricing tiers always win"
- ".99 always wins"
- "countdown timers always increase sales"
- "more funnel steps always work"
- "fewer choices always work"

# 29. Ethical Persuasion Standard

Allowed:
- clear recommendations
- real progress
- transparent comparisons
- real proof
- relevant upsells
- accurate premium positioning
- clear decline paths
- real deadlines

Not allowed:
- fake scarcity
- fake social proof
- hidden paid defaults
- prechecked upsells
- fake discounts
- disguised charges
- misleading previews
- guilt-based decline language

---

# 30. Research-Derived Funnel Rules

1. Each page gets one psychological job.
2. Lead with the next problem, not the next product.
3. Use real progress.
4. Preserve autonomy.
5. Fulfill before reselling.
6. Keep the free starter genuinely useful.
7. Segment by verified behavior.
8. Keep checkout minimal.
9. Do not add offers just to lengthen the funnel.
10. Optimize the weakest transition.
11. Increase proof as price rises.
12. Increase differentiation as price rises.
13. Do not let premium value depend only on page count.
14. Do not use fake savings to bridge a weak premium offer.
15. Master Collection must be meaningfully broader than the $49 system.

# 33. Final Principle

The upgraded funnel should not become more aggressive because prices are higher.

It should become **more convincing, more transparent, and more differentiated**.

```text
FREE
"I understand this."

↓
$4.99
"I trust this."

↓
$19.99
"I want broader value."

↓
$49
"I want the complete core system."

↓
$79–$99
"I want the master collection."
```

**Higher price requires stronger value clarity, not stronger pressure.**
